Built for AlpaSim   /   Configurable scenario engine

From recorded drives to automated “what-if” tests.

We developed a route-aware scenario engine inside AlpaSim that turns a local recorded scene into a controlled driving experiment. Define a pedestrian crossing or passenger-car intrusion, specify its route position, motion and activation gap, and the engine automatically triggers the encounter as the ego vehicle approaches.

TransFuser-K1 then drives the vehicle in a real feedback loop: physics updates its state, Omnidreams generates the next camera view, and the system records what happened. This moves evaluation beyond passive replay to measurable reactions to scripted hazards.

What directors should know

Scenario automation is working. Safety outcomes are mixed.

The platform now creates and executes targeted encounters without manually timing each event. In initial trials, K1 stopped for a car obstruction; both pedestrian cases still ended in geometric collisions.

Advance with validation gates

Current boundary: one scripted encounter per run, in one local scene and front camera—not a general multi-agent traffic generator.

The strategic takeaway

This is now an instrument for discovering failure modes—not a certification that those modes are solved.

Interpretation discipline

A passing scene score is not perfect route imitation. A low score may reflect safe waiting: the car scenario scored 0.8174 because stopping reduced progress. Incident counts and clearances matter alongside the aggregate score.

02   /   What was unlocked

A scenario engine, not just a simulation connection.

We added a configuration-driven layer for controlled encounters: route-relative crossing and stop positions, ego-distance triggers, deterministic movement profiles and ground-aware actor placement. A scenario is registered up front, then activated automatically at the appropriate point in the run.

That actor is represented consistently in AlpaSim state, renderer conditioning, logs and collision evaluation. K1 controls ego; controller and physics advance the vehicle; Omnidreams generates the next visual observation. This permits direct testing of the consequences of learned decisions rather than passively replaying a fixed future.

03   /   Strongest positive evidence

When a car blocks the lane, K1 stops.

Car-intrusion and no-car runs used the same scene, front camera, parked-vehicle setting and recorded renderer seed.

A measurable braking response—not a scripted safety intervention.

The injected car moved toward and into the ego route over four seconds, then remained stopped. K1 progressively shortened its plan, decelerated and came to rest. The matched control continued moving.

Important fidelity limit

Stopping is proven here. An oncoming cut-in is not.

Generated pixels make the car appear rear-facing, even though the scripted geometry faces ego. The strongest defensible claim is K1 braking for a visible stopped-car obstruction.

The standard score fell to 0.8174 (vs. control 1.0) because the evaluator penalized reduced route progress despite zero collision and offroad incidents.

05   /   Evidence boundaries

Know exactly what we can—and cannot—claim.

The evidence is local, replayable and specific; it should not be generalized beyond the tested configuration.

Established

Evidence-backed capabilities

  • The K1 → controller/physics → generated-observation loop executes end to end.
  • Speed, calibration and evaluation defects were located and corrected.
  • Scripted actors agree across canonical state, logs and renderer conditioning.
  • Fresh RGB inputs influence K1 predictions; exact plan replay is possible.
  • The car obstruction elicited learned braking and a collision-free stop.
Still unproven

Safety and fidelity claims

  • Reliable pedestrian avoidance or robust hazard negotiation.
  • Exact rendering of actor identity, orientation, gait or pixel placement.
  • Generalization across scenes, cameras or checkpoints.
  • Reactive behavior from surrounding recorded traffic.
  • Complete causal separation of perception, renderer and planner failures.
06   /   Recommended director decision

Proceed—but make validation the next deliverable.

This project is ready to support targeted learning and risk discovery, not a headline claim of general driving safety.

Authorize a gated experimental phase that turns promising isolated behavior into repeatable, interpretable evidence.

Decision boundary: preserve the working integration, prioritize visual validity and repeatability, and defer broad performance claims until across-scenario evidence improves. No timeline or funding estimate is inferred from the engineering report.

01

Gate the generated scene

Check actor presence, orientation, location and temporal persistence against scripted conditioning before scoring policy behavior.

02

Repeat controlled encounters

Use matched seeds and change one factor at a time—approach side, speed, gap and visibility. Pair results with exact K1 image-input replays.

03

Expand only after trust

Broaden local scenes and checkpoint coverage once contracts and render fidelity hold; treat reactive surrounding traffic as separate future scope.

Proposed director-level measures: visual validity · collision / offroad · minimum clearance · response timing · cross-seed consistency.
